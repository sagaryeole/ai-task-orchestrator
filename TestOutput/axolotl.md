The axolotl is a remarkable aquatic salamander native to Mexico's Lake Xochimilco. Unlike most amphibians, axolotls retain larval features via neoteny. They keep external gills filtering oxygen, growing up to eighteen inches with colors from wild-type brown to golden albino. Their extraordinary ability is regeneration; an axolotl can regrow entire limbs, heart portions, and spinal cord sections without scarring. Scientists study them for regenerative medicine breakthroughs. In captivity, they need cool, clean water and a diet of worms, brine shrimp, and small fish. Unfortunately, axolotls are critically endangered due to habitat destruction, prompting worldwide conservation to protect these gentle ambassadors of biodiversity.
